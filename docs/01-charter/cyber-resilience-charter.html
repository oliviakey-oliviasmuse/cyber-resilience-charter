1.2 The Business Case — Cyber Risk as a Process Defect

Traditional cybersecurity focuses on perimeter defence. This framework reframes cyber incidents as manufacturing process defects that directly degrade Overall Equipment Effectiveness (OEE), yield, and Cost of Poor Quality (COPQ). In EV cell manufacturing, a single digital stoppage of a continuous chemical or precision-formation stage produces scrap that cannot be recovered and, in worst cases, life-safety events that cannot be insured.

[ Traditional IT Security ] ──> Vulnerabilities, Threats, Perimeter
[ MBB Cyber-Lean Model    ] ──> Defect Rate, Cycle Time (MTTR), Financial COPQ

The shift is not cosmetic. It repositions cyber resilience from a technical control function to an operational excellence programme with measurable capability, statistical control, and quantified financial protection.

2.1 Recap of the P × E × M Model

This engagement reuses the financial exposure model established in the Operational Stability Infrastructure (OSI) whitepaper:

Exposure         = P × E
Protected Value  = Exposure × M

Where: - P = Annual production value per department (anchored at $200M per OSI model) - E = Erosion/exposure band (probability-adjusted annual loss) - M = Mitigation capture rate (achievable fraction of exposure avoided)

For the OSI whitepaper, the entropy-driven erosion band was modelled at 3–8% of production value. For this engagement, the cyber exposure band (E_cyber) is a probability-adjusted annualised loss across five distinct exposure classes — direct downtime, silent corruption, IP exfiltration, regulatory breach, and safety liability — most of which have no analogue in the entropy framework.

2.2 Cyber Exposure Decomposition (E_cyber)

E_cyber is the sum of five independently modelled exposure classes. Each is anchored to industry-published data and to the engagement's anonymised baseline (validated in Measure phase, Tollgate G2).

2.2.1 Direct Downtime Exposure (E_downtime)

Parameter Value Source
Mean downtime per major manufacturing ransomware event 5–21 days Dragos 2024, Coveware Q1 2024
Cell line direct cost per hour $250K–$500K+ modelled (blended scrap + idle labour + recovery + contractual penalty)
Per-incident direct cost (high-volume cell line) $30M–$100M+ modelled

This is the "loud" failure class — the event triggers a halt, gets detected, and triggers the IR cycle. It is the exposure the MBB framework's IR/SMED recovery rig is designed to protect.

2.2.2 Silent Corruption Exposure (E_silent_corruption) — NEW

Silent corruption is the structurally more dangerous failure class: process parameters (formation profiles, coating weights, dosing volumes, calibration constants) are altered by cyber compromise without triggering a halt. The line continues to run. Cells pass in-line inspection. The defect is detected only in the field, in warranty returns, or in thermal events.

Frequency (Tier-1 cell supplier, high IP-value target) ~1 in 2–5 years (higher than overt attacks) modelled; quiet attacks are more frequent because they evade immediate detection
Per-event cost: recall $10M–$100M+ industry benchmarks (battery recall precedents)
Per-event cost: warranty extension $5M–$50M industry
Per-event cost: OEM contractual breach (product, not regulatory) $5M–$50M per OEM supplier contract terms
Per-event cost: yield loss (corrupted batch already shipped) $5M–$30M modelled
Blended per-event cost $25M–$200M+ sum of above
Probability-adjusted annual silent corruption exposure $5M–$50M / year / department = (per-event cost × annualised frequency)

Silent corruption is the failure class that justifies the FMEA's Severity = 9–10 rating for parameter-tampering failure modes, and it is the structural reason the AI-augmented Layer 3 (behavioural anomaly detection, parameter drift baselining) carries equal weight to the IR cycle. Detecting silent corruption within MTTD ≤ 30 min is the binding capability target; the alternative is a recall event that costs 5–10× the IR cycle.

2.2.3 IP Exfiltration Exposure (E_ip)

IP exfiltration is the largest single exposure class and the one most resistant to insurance transfer. It is also the dominant motivation for state-aligned APT targeting of EV cell manufacturers. The widened valuation band ($100M–$1B+) reflects proprietary solid-state, sodium-ion, and advanced dopant systems where a single platform's chemistry IP routinely trades at the upper end of the range in M&A and licensing transactions.

Regulatory exposure is increasing, not decreasing. The EU NIS2 transposition deadline (October 2024) has passed; OEMs have begun enforcing cyber attestation clauses in 2024–2025 supplier contracts. The exposure band assumes a baseline of full compliance, which is currently the binding constraint in the engagement.

Safety liability is the existential tail risk. A single cyber-induced thermal runaway event in a populated area is a brand-destroying, potentially fatal, criminal-liability event. It cannot be insured to its full potential cost. The exposure band is modelled as a low-probability tail, but the tail itself is the reason the engagement carries Severity = 10 in the FMEA.

2.3 Total Cyber Exposure Band

Summing the five exposure classes:

E_cyber = E_downtime + E_silent_corruption + E_ip + E_regulatory + E_safety
        = ($4M–$20M) + ($5M–$50M) + ($10M–$100M) + ($1M–$10M) + ($5M–$unbounded)
        = $20M–$180M+ / year / department  (excluding unbounded safety tail)
        = $20M–$unbounded / year / department  (with safety tail)

For a single department at P = $200M annual production value, the cyber exposure band is $20M–$180M+/year — 3–11× the OSI entropy framework's $6M–$16M band.

The upper bound is dominated by IP exfiltration ($100M) and silent corruption ($50M). The safety tail is structurally uncapped and treated as a separate governance concern.

2.4 Cost of Poor Quality (COPQ) Estimate

COPQ is the engagement-level operational measure of waste created by cyber-induced defects, expressed in $ / minute / line. The COPQ band below is what the CISO sees on the executive dashboard's second tile.

COPQ Component Lower Bound ($/min/line) Upper Bound ($/min/line) Basis
Direct scrap (slurry, electrolyte, half-cells, formation cycling) $1,500 $4,000 modelled from cell BOM + scrap yield
Idle labour (line operators, GBs, support) $800 $2,000 modelled from staffing × loaded rate
Recovery cost (forensics, restoration, validation) $500 $2,500 modelled
Contractual penalty (OEM contractual breach, missed shipment) $1,000 $3,000 modelled from supplier terms
Blended COPQ (high-volume cell line) $4,000/min $11,500/min = $240K–$690K per hour

The blended COPQ is the financial translation of a single minute of cyber-induced downtime. It is the operational anchor for the MTTR targets in §1.4.1: every minute of MTTR above target is $4K–$11.5K of additional COPQ per line per event.

Note: Silent corruption is not captured in the blended COPQ band, because silent corruption is by definition a non-downtime event. The COPQ is the downtime-exposure operational measure. Silent corruption is captured in the E_silent_corruption exposure band (§2.2.2) and surfaces on the dashboard as a separate tile tracking recall/warranty cost avoidance.

2.8 Sensitivity Analysis

The exposure band in §2.3 has three primary sensitivities:

  1. IP valuation band (E_ip): A 2× increase in chemistry IP valuation (plausible for proprietary solid-state or sodium-ion platforms) raises the upper bound of E_ip beyond $100M/year. The engagement's protected value scales linearly with IP valuation.
  2. Safety tail binding: Any single realised safety event in the industry materially shifts the regulatory and OEM-contractual exposure bands. The post-event regulatory environment is more punitive than the pre-event environment.
  3. OEM contractual enforcement: As VW / BMW / Stellantis tighten cyber attestation enforcement in 2025–2027 supplier contracts, the E_regulatory band widens. Early engagement de-risks this trajectory.

The Measure phase (Tollgate G2) will calibrate the actual baseline against the engagement's own anonymised data, and the targets in §1.4 will adjust accordingly.

3.1 Role Architecture

The engagement is structured around five role layers. Each layer has explicit decision rights, escalation ownership, and accountability boundaries.

3.1.1 CISO — Executive Sponsor & Process Owner

The CISO is the single accountable authority for the engagement. The CISO's role is structural, not operational — they set the boundaries within which the MBB and the 19 Green Belts operate.

Decision rights: - Risk appetite — defines what residual risk is acceptable post-engagement; sets the Z-score target floor - Confidentiality protocol — owns the data-access governance (§3.5); grants or revokes the 19 Green Belts' access to anonymised data - Tollgate sign-off — sole signatory on G1–G5; no phase advances without CISO approval - Budget allocation — approves any non-standard spend (e.g. external SOC tooling, AI agent platform licensing) - Strategic escalation — receives Tier 3 and Tier 4 escalations (§3.4); routes to executive committee as required - Incident notification — receives real-time notification of any confirmed cyber event on a protected line, regardless of severity

Accountability boundaries: - Does NOT make engineering or methodological decisions (those belong to the MBB) - Does NOT allocate Green Belt capacity across tracks (that belongs to the MBB) - Does NOT participate in daily execution (that belongs to the Track Leads and Green Belts)

3.1.2 MBB — Project Lead & Methodology Owner

The MBB (Olivia) is the accountable authority for engagement delivery and methodology integrity. The MBB's role is to translate cyber risk into DMAIC vocabulary, govern project velocity, and protect the 19 Green Belts' capacity.

Decision rights: - Project velocity — drives the engagement to each tollgate on schedule; resolves cross-track dependencies - DMAIC methodology — owns the analytical engine (FMEA, SPC, capability indices); arbitrates methodological disputes - Track allocation — allocates the 19 Green Belts across Track A / B / C; can reallocate within the approved headcount - Internal escalation — resolves Tier 1 and Tier 2 issues (§3.4) within 24 hours - Operational decisions — makes all day-to-day decisions within the approved charter; only escalates to CISO when scope, budget, or risk appetite is at issue - CISO presentation — sole presenter of tollgate findings to the CISO

Accountability boundaries: - Does NOT set risk appetite (CISO) - Does NOT override CISO confidentiality decisions (CISO) - Does NOT approve budget outside charter (CISO) - Does NOT make line-level operational decisions that affect production schedule (Operations Director)

3.1.3 Track Leads — Track A / B / C

Each of the three tracks has a Track Lead (typically the most senior Green Belt on the track). Track Leads are responsible for execution within their track.

Track allocation: - Track A — Architecture (6 GBs) — IT/OT segmentation, air-gapping, network isolation, vendor remote-access hardening - Track B — Data & Metrics (7 GBs) — anonymised data pipelines, SPC chart construction, FMEA calculation, capability reporting - Track C — Automation (6 GBs) — Cyber Poka-Yoke coding, SMED recovery rig, IR playbook automation, AI-augmented Layer 3 agents

Decision rights: - Track execution — drives the track's work plan within the tollgate scope - Track-internal escalation — resolves Tier 1 issues within the track - Cross-track coordination — works with MBB on cross-track dependencies (e.g. Track A segmentation → Track C Poka-Yoke enforcement) - Technical decisions — makes engineering decisions within the track's approved scope

Accountability boundaries: - Does NOT allocate work across tracks (MBB) - Does NOT make decisions affecting other tracks without MBB coordination - Does NOT modify the data-access protocol (CISO)

3.1.4 Green Belts (19 total)

The Green Belts are the execution team. They write code, query data, deploy automation, and execute the standard work defined in §6.

Decision rights: - Code / data / automation — write code within the track's approved scope, query the anonymised data lake, deploy automation - Standard work — follow the Standard Work checklist (§6) for weekly, monthly, quarterly routines - Issue flag — raise issues to the Track Lead; can pause work if a data-access anomaly is detected

Accountability boundaries: - Cannot query raw data; cannot modify the anonymisation pipeline; cannot share query results outside the track - Cannot make architectural decisions (Track Lead / MBB) - Cannot communicate directly with CISO without MBB coordination

4.4.2 Cyber-FMEA Methodology

The Cyber-FMEA follows the standard LSS MBB scoring:

RPN = S × O × D
  • S (Severity, 1–10): Impact if the failure mode occurs. S = 10 for any failure mode that can cause loss of life, regulatory shutdown, or existential business impact (silent corruption in formation; safety-critical system compromise).

  • D (Detection, 1–10): Difficulty of detecting the failure mode before it causes harm. D = 9–10 for silent corruption (no halt, no inspection failure); D = 4–6 for overt cyber events (SIEM/SOC visibility).

4.5.3 Cyber Poka-Yoke (Mistake-Proofing)

Cyber Poka-Yoke is the structural enforcement layer. Unlike Digital 5C (which is hygiene), Poka-Yoke makes certain failure modes structurally impossible.

Poka-Yoke design principles:

  1. Hard-coded network isolation: Critical line PLCs reject any inbound traffic that does not originate from a locally authenticated, cryptographic handshake. No software-only firewall rule; enforced at the PLC firmware level.
  2. One-way data diodes on safety-critical paths (formation cycling, electrolyte injection) — data can flow out for monitoring, but no command can flow in.
  3. Cryptographic recipe signing: All process recipes (formation profiles, coating weights, dosing volumes, aging cycles) are cryptographically signed. PLCs reject unsigned or modified recipes.
  4. Vendor remote access through authenticated broker: No direct VPN access for vendors. All access is brokered, time-bounded, session-recorded, and cryptographically authenticated.
  5. Parameter drift interlocks: Real-time comparison of process parameters against the signed baseline recipe. Drift beyond a defined threshold triggers automatic line isolation.

4.5.4 SMED for Disaster Recovery

SMED (Single-Minute Exchange of Die) is repurposed from manufacturing changeover to cyber recovery. The goal is to convert "internal" recovery steps (performed during the attack, under time pressure) into "external" steps (pre-staged, ready to deploy).

Internal (during attack) → External (pre-staged)
Search for backup files → Immutable, pre-verified offline backups on hot-swappable drives
Configure recovery servers → Pre-configured standalone restore rigs, cryptographically verified
Validate recovered systems → Pre-validated golden images, signed and offline
Authorise recovery actions → Pre-authorised recovery runbooks with CISO standing approval for Severity = 8+ events
Re-establish vendor access → Vendor access re-established only after clean-state validation

SMED target: MTTR ≤ 1 hour for hard stop (ransomware/wiper), validated in the G4 red-team exercise (Six Sigma target).

6.1 Continuation Philosophy

The 12-week engagement (Weeks 1–12) is the initial structural push. The continuation phase (Week 13 onwards) is what makes the engagement operational infrastructure rather than a one-time project. Without continuation, the engagement is vulnerable to entropy relapse — exactly the failure mode the OSI whitepaper's Layer 4 (Governance & Entropy Defence) was designed to prevent.

Three continuation disciplines:

  1. Green Belt Standard Work — embedded weekly / monthly / quarterly / annual routines that the 19 GBs execute at 10% weekly capacity
  2. Cyber-Kaizen Triggers — pre-defined conditions that initiate immediate improvement events without waiting for scheduled reviews
  3. Maturity Scoring — quarterly self-assessment against a 5-level model; capability regression triggers escalation

The continuation phase is governed by §7 (CISO Governance Cycle) and protected by §8 (Constraints & Risks).

B.3 Color Coding & Alert System

The dashboard uses a 3-tier color coding system, consistent across all tiles:

Color Status Definition CISO Notification
🟢 GREEN On target All metrics within engagement target; no open issues Standard cadence (bi-weekly briefing)
🟡 AMBER Drift One or more sub-process below target; open Kaizen > 5 BD; FMEA refresh overdue; or other warning signals Bi-weekly briefing escalates; MBB + Track Lead investigate within 48h
🔴 RED Critical Engagement Z-score < 4.0; Tier 2/3 escalation open; structural change trigger (M&A / IP licensing / supply chain); or safety event CISO notified within 4h; emergency briefing within 24h; engagement re-scope possible

Dashboard-level status: Worst-status of any tile, with engagement Z-score as the headline number. A single RED tile flips the dashboard to RED.

B.4 Interactive Features

The dashboard is interactive, with the following features:

Feature Description
Drill-down Click any tile metric to view anonymised source data (per access control)
Time range Toggle between 12-week, 24-week, 52-week views
Export CISO and MBB can export tile data as PDF or CSV; logged in audit trail
Annotations MBB and Track Leads can add annotations to metrics (e.g. "W7 spike due to planned maintenance")
Alerts Configurable per-tile alerts via email or push notification; RED status triggers immediate alert
Mobile Responsive design; CISO can view on tablet/phone with same access controls
Audit log Every dashboard view, drill-down, export, annotation is logged; quarterly CISO review
